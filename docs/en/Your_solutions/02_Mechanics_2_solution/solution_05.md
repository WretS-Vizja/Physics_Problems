## 5. Inelastic Collision
 
A 70 kg runner moving at $3 \text{ m/s}$ jumps onto a 140 kg stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.
 
> **Problem**
>
> $m_1 = 70$ kg at $v_1 = 3$ m/s jumps onto $m_2 = 140$ kg at rest. Find $v_f$ and check if $KE$ is conserved.
 
> **Idea**
>
> When the runner jumps onto the cart and they move together, this is a perfectly inelastic collision. Momentum is conserved because no external horizontal force acts on the runner+cart system. After finding the final speed, we compare kinetic energies before and after to check whether KE is conserved.
 
> **Step 1 – Conservation of momentum**
>
> Before: runner has momentum $p_1 = m_1 v_1 = 70 \times 3 = 210$ kg·m/s; cart is at rest ($p_2 = 0$).
>
> After: combined system has mass $(m_1 + m_2) = 210$ kg moving at $v_f$.
>
> $$m_1 v_1 + m_2 \cdot 0 = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$
>
> $$v_f = \frac{m_1 v_1}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{70 \times 3}{70 + 140} = \frac{210}{210} = 1 \text{ m/s}$$
>
> **Note:** The total mass is exactly 3 times the runner's mass, so the speed drops to exactly $1/3$ of the runner's original speed.
 
> **Step 2 – Kinetic energy comparison**
>
> Initial kinetic energy (only the runner is moving):
>
> $$KE_i = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 70 \times 3^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 70 \times 9 = 315 \text{ J}$$
>
> Final kinetic energy (runner + cart moving together):
>
> $$KE_f = \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)v_f^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 210 \times 1^2 = 105 \text{ J}$$
 
> **Step 3 – Energy loss analysis**
>
> $$\Delta KE = KE_i - KE_f = 315 - 105 = 210 \text{ J}$$
>
> As a fraction: $\dfrac{KE_f}{KE_i} = \dfrac{105}{315} = \dfrac{1}{3} \approx 33.3\%$
>
> Only $1/3$ of the original kinetic energy survives; $2/3$ (= 210 J) is dissipated.
>
> **Where does the lost energy go?** During the collision, the runner's feet interact with the cart surface. The "sticking" process involves internal friction, deformation of shoes/cart surface, sound waves, and heating.
>
> **General formula for energy loss in perfectly inelastic collisions with a stationary target:**
>
> $$\frac{\Delta KE}{KE_i} = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$$
>
> Here: $\frac{140}{210} = \frac{2}{3}$, which confirms our calculation.
 
> **Result**
>
> $$\boxed{v_f = 1 \text{ m/s}}$$
>
> Kinetic energy is **not conserved**. This is a perfectly inelastic collision. Out of the initial 315 J, only 105 J remains; 210 J of energy (two-thirds) is dissipated as heat, sound, and deformation. Perfectly inelastic collisions always lose the maximum possible kinetic energy while still obeying momentum conservation.
