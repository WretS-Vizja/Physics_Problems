## 6. Energy Dissipation
 
A tennis ball is dropped from a height of $2.0$ m. After each bounce, it loses 30% of its mechanical energy. To what height does it rise after the second bounce?
 
> **Problem**
>
> $h_0 = 2.0$ m, 30% energy loss per bounce. Find $h_2$ after the second bounce.
 
> **Idea**
>
> At the top of each arc, the ball is momentarily at rest, so all its mechanical energy is gravitational potential energy: $E = mgh$. Since $E \propto h$ (with $m$ and $g$ constant), losing 30% of energy means the height after each bounce is multiplied by 0.70. This is a geometric sequence.
 
> **Step 1 – After the first bounce**
>
> Before the first bounce, the ball has energy $E_0 = mgh_0$. After the bounce, it retains 70%:
>
> $$E_1 = 0.70 \times E_0 = 0.70 \times mgh_0 = mg(0.70 \times h_0)$$
>
> So the new height is:
>
> $$h_1 = 0.70 \times h_0 = 0.70 \times 2.0 = 1.40 \text{ m}$$
>
> **Physical note:** The 30% energy loss occurs during the brief contact with the ground. The ball deforms, internal friction generates heat, and sound is emitted.
 
> **Step 2 – After the second bounce**
>
> The same process repeats: the ball loses another 30% during the second bounce.
>
> $$h_2 = 0.70 \times h_1 = 0.70 \times 1.40 = 0.98 \text{ m}$$
>
> Or, recognizing the geometric pattern directly:
>
> $$h_n = h_0 \times (0.70)^n$$
>
> For $n = 2$:
>
> $$h_2 = 2.0 \times (0.70)^2 = 2.0 \times 0.49 = 0.98 \text{ m}$$
>
> **Continuing the pattern:** After the 3rd bounce: $h_3 = 2.0 \times 0.70^3 = 0.686$ m. After the 10th: $h_{10} = 2.0 \times 0.70^{10} \approx 0.056$ m.
 
> **Result**
>
> $$\boxed{h_2 = 0.98 \text{ m}}$$
