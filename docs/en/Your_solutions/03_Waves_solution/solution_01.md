# 1. Wave Properties

## Problem
A sound wave in air has a frequency of 440 Hz. If the speed of sound in air is 343 m/s, what is its wavelength? What is its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is 1482 m/s?

## Solution

**Step 1 — Relevant equation.**  
For any wave, the wavelength, frequency, and propagation speed are related by

$$v = f\lambda \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

**Step 2 — Wavelength in air.**  

$$\lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{v_{\text{air}}}{f} = \frac{343\ \text{m/s}}{440\ \text{Hz}} \approx 0.780\ \text{m}$$

**Step 3 — Wavelength in water.**  
The source frequency does not change when the wave enters a new medium; only the speed (and therefore the wavelength) changes.

$$\lambda_{\text{water}} = \frac{v_{\text{water}}}{f} = \frac{1482\ \text{m/s}}{440\ \text{Hz}} \approx 3.368\ \text{m}$$

**Step 4 — Interpretation.**  
The wavelength is roughly $4.3\times$ larger in water because sound travels $\approx 4.3\times$ faster there.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\lambda_{\text{air}} \approx 0.78\ \text{m},\qquad \lambda_{\text{water}} \approx 3.37\ \text{m}}$$
