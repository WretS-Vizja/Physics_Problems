# 4. Phase Difference

## Problem
What is the phase difference in radians between two points on a wave that are separated by a distance of $\lambda/3$?

## Solution

**Step 1 — Phase as a function of position.**  
For a traveling wave $y(x,t) = A\sin(kx - \omega t)$, the phase at position $x$ and time $t$ is

$$\varphi(x,t) = kx - \omega t$$

At a fixed time $t$, the phase difference between two points depends only on their spatial separation $\Delta x$:

$$\Delta\varphi = k\,\Delta x$$

**Step 2 — Use the definition of wave number.**  

$$k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$$

This means that over one full wavelength $\lambda$, the phase advances by $2\pi$ rad — a complete cycle.

**Step 3 — Substitute $\Delta x = \lambda/3$.**  

$$\Delta\varphi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}\ \text{rad}$$

**Step 4 — Express in degrees (sanity check).**  

$$\frac{2\pi}{3}\ \text{rad} = 120°$$

This makes sense: one-third of a wavelength corresponds to one-third of a full cycle, i.e. one-third of $360°$.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\Delta\varphi = \frac{2\pi}{3}\ \text{rad} \approx 2.094\ \text{rad} \;(120°)}$$
